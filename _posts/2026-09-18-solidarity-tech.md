Ivan Pardo's [Solidarity Tech](https://www.solidarity.tech/platform) has an
important idea for organizing tools: communication between organizers and
workers should route through the tool. Organizers don't need to log that they
had a call, if making the call creates the record of the call.

The purpose of organizing tools is mainly to facilitate the management of an
organizing campaign in two senses. First, as a tool to gather information about
the readiness of a group of workers to take collective action--win a union
election, demonstrate strength for a contract campaign, go on strike. Second, to
facilitate the personnel management of organizers--are organizing staff doing
what they were assigned to do.

The data, for both purposes, has typically been created through organizers doing
data entry: logging contact attempts, conversations, assessments, event
participation, and demonstrations of support. Some indicators of workers's
readiness have been automated or pushed down to the workers themselves, as with
engagement tracking on digital communication or
[self checkin-in to events](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/32bj-stewards-leaders/id6739307031),
but data entry remains a big part of the work of organizers.

In Solidarity Tech, organizers call or text workers through the native apps that
Ivan has built for Android and iPhone. This technical decision means the
consequence that the fact that a organizer called or texted a workers can be
automatically logged.

The organizer still does have to do data entry--make notes about the call,
adjust assesments record commitments to events, but since the communicatation
happend through the app, Solidarity Tech is able to present a focused interface
for recording the information about the communication that just happened or is
ongoing.

The information that the organizer still has to record is now mainly the
information the campaign needs to understand the state of the campaign. The
information that is automatically created is what an organizing lead needs to
manage personnel, at least at a basic level.

Now, this system worked really well for Rideshare Drivers United, when most
organzing conversations were happening on the phone and text. If the
conversation happened in-person. then an organizer has to log the whole
interaction manually. But, even in campaigns when there are lots of face-to-face
conversations, there are usally lots of conversations over phone and text too.
Might as well make those easier.

## Tools and Campaigns

The other important idea that I take from Solidarity Tech is that its strengths
are due to its specificity. Ivan started developing the tool for a particular
campaign, and its strongest ideas--integrated communications, first-class
support for peer-to-peer organizing--can be traced directly to needs of of the
workers building [Rideshare Drivers United](https://www.drivers-united.org/)
over many years.

We should do this more.

Every campaign and union does not need to build its own organizing tool, but
maybe a lot of them should. If we had more particular tools, that would make our
general tools better, but it would also help us explore new models in campaign
structure and model.
